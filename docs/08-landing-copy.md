## The spine

> **We can see things you cannot.**

**Ratified by Jon, 19 August 2026.** It is not a line that appears on the page. It
is the job the top of the page has to do.

**The reasoning, because it decides the order of everything.** The mark most
visitors came for, Claude's text watermark, **can never be shown.** It is not a
character with a position, it is the word sequence itself. So the arrival screen
cannot prove their watermark and must stop trying.

**What it can prove is that we see the invisible.** Three characters pulled out of
a paragraph, in front of them, in a second. **Then the third row tells them the one
they came for is certain: if Claude wrote this, it is marked.**

**Sight, then certainty.** The reader watches us see what they cannot, and then
learns the mark they actually care about is not a maybe. **Why nobody can point at
it sits one layer down, behind the +**, where it explains why the answer is a
rewrite rather than a search.

**Rejected, and why.** Leading with Claude directly. **Claude signs the images it
generates and that is provable and dramatic**, `04` entry 68, but **Jon ruled it
out: "most didn't come here for images."** A Claude demonstration exists and it is
the wrong demonstration for this audience.

## State 1: arrival, the example is loaded

**Where:** the hero box, visible without scrolling on both desktop and mobile.
Confirmed live by Jon, 19 August 2026, and it stays that way.

### Inside the box

**The example paragraph. Rewritten on Jon's instruction: "make it a funny paragraph
about some bullshit."** The old one was a corporate memo about a committee approving
a rollout, which is nobody's world and least of all a student's.

> The committee has reviewed the incident and agreed that Gary from accounts will
> not be organising the Christmas party again. Costs are held at 4.2 million,
> unchanged from the February estimate. Gary maintains that the alpacas were a
> gift, and that eleven of them was a reasonable number for a venue of that size.

**The bureaucratic deadpan is kept, because the contrast is the joke. Only the
subject changed.** It still carries a figure, a date and a written-out number, so
the facts-survive guarantee has something to prove itself on. **The same three
characters are planted in it:** a narrow no-break space, a zero width space and a
no-break space.

**One line, directly under the text, and it is the most important sentence on the
screen:**

> **Three characters are hiding in this paragraph. You are looking straight at
> them.**

**Why it exists.** Without it the marks read as cursor artefacts or a rendering
fault. **The connection between "those little bars" and "hidden characters: 3
found" is the entire payoff of the screen and it is currently left to be
inferred.**

**Open, not written:** tapping a mark should say what it is. "A narrow no-break
space. ChatGPT emits these." **Turns three mystery bars into three discoveries.**

**Settled, Jon, 19 August 2026. The technical labels stay.** The assistant proposed

from hidden characters, metadata, and statistical watermark, we're creating a
technical deviation."*

**He is right, and it is worth writing down.** These are the real names. They are
what a visitor meets in a news article, in Anthropic's own documentation, and in
anything else they read about this. **Inventing our own names would build a private
vocabulary that connects to nothing outside this page.** The label stays technical
and **the teaching moves to the status line and the +**, which is where it
belongs.

### Behind the + on each row

**Row 1, Hidden characters**

> Characters with no width, sitting between the words where nothing shows on the
> page. Tools like ChatGPT emit them and they survive copy and paste. We find every
> one and show you exactly where it was.

*Unchanged. It already works.*

**Row 2, Metadata**

> Data hidden inside a file rather than in the words you can read: what made it,
> when, and often a signed record naming the tool. Claude signs the images it
> generates. Pasted text has no file around it, so this one sits out.

*Unchanged. It already works.*

**Row 3, Statistical watermark. Rewritten, and ratified by Jon this session.**

> **It is not hidden in your words. It is your words.** The exact order Claude
> chose them in.
>
> Another AI swaps a few and leaves the rest alone. Whatever it leaves alone still
> carries the mark.
>
> **We rebuild every sentence. Three words in a row is the most that survives, and
> your facts and length are checked against your original.**

**The row's status line, ratified by Jon as a replacement for both PRESENT and for
the assistant's proposed "Nothing can show you this one":**

> **If Claude wrote this, it is marked.**

**Was a layout problem, now fixed.** Seven words measured 223px of a 375px phone
screen: the label wrapped to two lines and the status printed straight through it.
**Below `sm` the label and status now stack**, label over status. From `sm` up the
row is unchanged.

**Why this replaced PRESENT.** The old badge was triggered by the input containing
words. Nothing was examined. **It asserted a finding about the visitor's specific
text that we cannot make.** This line makes the same point at the population level,
where it is simply true, and it survives the day Anthropic's detector opens. **It
also sells harder than the alternative**: it removes any doubt that the visitor has
a problem, rather than describing our own limits. `04` entry 81.

**The one line version, for anywhere too tight for three:**

> **The mark is the word sequence itself. Another AI leaves most of it alone. We
> rebuild every sentence.**
