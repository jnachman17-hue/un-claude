Northwind Logistics reported revenue of $4.2 million for the third quarter of 2025, an increase of eighteen percent against the same period a year earlier. The company said the gain was driven almost entirely by its enterprise segment, which grew 34 percent while the consumer division contracted by roughly 6 percent. Chief executive Marta Delacroix described the quarter as the strongest since the company listed in March 2021.

The enterprise result was helped by three contracts signed in July, the largest of which was a five-year agreement with Bergstrom Manufacturing worth an estimated $11.8 million over its full term. Analysts had expected revenue between $3.9 million and $4.1 million, so the reported figure came in above the top of that range. Shares rose 7 percent in after-hours trading before giving back about half of the gain the following morning.

Operating costs also rose, though more slowly than revenue. Total operating expenses reached $3.1 million, up 11 percent year on year, with most of the increase attributable to headcount. Northwind employed 412 people at the end of September, compared with 361 twelve months earlier. Delacroix said the company expected hiring to slow in the fourth quarter and that headcount would likely finish the year below 430.

The board declared a dividend of twelve cents per share, payable on 14 November to shareholders of record as of 31 October. It is the fourth consecutive quarter in which Northwind has paid a dividend, and the payout ratio now stands at approximately 22 percent of net income. Chief financial officer Peter Okonkwo said the board intended to keep the ratio below 30 percent while the company continues to invest in its logistics network.

Northwind also disclosed a one-off charge of $480,000 relating to the closure of its Rotterdam warehouse, which ceased operations on 30 June. The company said the closure would reduce annual fixed costs by around $1.3 million from 2026 onward, and that the affected 47 staff had either been redeployed within the group or offered severance. Okonkwo said the charge was fully reflected in the third-quarter accounts and that no further costs relating to the closure were expected.

Looking ahead, the company guided to full-year revenue of between $16.5 million and $17.2 million, which would represent growth of between 14 and 19 percent on the prior year. That guidance assumes no material deterioration in freight rates, which fell 9 percent across the industry during the quarter according to the Baltic Dry Index. Delacroix cautioned that the fourth quarter is seasonally weaker for the consumer division and that the company was not assuming a recovery there before the second half of 2026.

Several analysts raised price targets following the announcement. Kestrel Research moved its target from $18 to $21 while maintaining a hold rating, citing improved margins but continued softness in consumer demand. Harbourline Capital was more positive, upgrading the stock to buy with a target of $24, and argued that the enterprise pipeline was being undervalued by the market. Two firms left their ratings unchanged.

The company will host its annual investor day in Chicago on 3 December, where management is expected to outline a three-year plan including a target of $25 million in annual revenue by 2028. Delacroix has said previously that reaching that figure would require at least two acquisitions, though Northwind has not completed one since acquiring Palermo Freight for $2.4 million in August 2023.
