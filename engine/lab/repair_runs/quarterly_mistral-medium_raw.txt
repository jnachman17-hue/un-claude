Northwind Logistics announced third-quarter earnings of $4.9 million for 2025, marking a 15 percent jump from the equivalent quarter in 2024. According to the firm, this surge came mainly from its enterprise operations, which expanded by 30 percent, while the consumer side shrank by about 5 percent. CEO Marta Delacroix called this the best quarterly performance since the company went public in March 2019.

A major factor behind the enterprise segment’s growth was three new contracts inked in July, including a five-year deal with Bergstrom Manufacturing valued at approximately $12.8 million over its lifetime. Market expectations had placed revenue forecasts in the $4.6 million to $4.8 million range, but the actual result exceeded the upper end. The stock climbed 6 percent in extended trading, though it later retreated by roughly half that gain the next day.

Expenses climbed as well, though at a slower pace than income. Operating costs totaled $3.6 million, a 10 percent rise from the prior year, with staffing accounting for the bulk of the increase. Employee numbers stood at 437 by the end of September, up from 381 a year before. Delacroix noted that recruitment would likely decelerate in the final quarter, with the workforce expected to remain under 470 by year-end.

The directors approved an 11-cent-per-share dividend, set for payment on 14 November to those registered as shareholders by 31 October. This marks the fourth straight quarterly dividend from Northwind Logistics, bringing the payout ratio to roughly 21 percent of net earnings. CFO Peter Okonkwo indicated the board’s plan to maintain this ratio below 31 percent as the company continues expanding its infrastructure.

Additionally, Northwind Logistics recorded a $495,000 exceptional cost tied to shutting down its Rotterdam facility, which closed its doors on 30 June. The move is projected to trim yearly fixed expenses by about $1.5 million starting in 2026. The company confirmed that all 43 impacted employees were either reassigned within the business or received severance packages. Okonkwo stated that the full impact of the charge was already accounted for in the third-quarter financials.

The firm projected annual earnings falling in the range of $18.5 million to $19.2 million, marking an increase of 13 to 19 percent compared with the previous twelve months. Chief executive Marta Delacroix noted that the final three months typically bring softer performance in the retail segment, with no anticipated rebound until after mid-2027. The most recent purchase made by the business was Palermo Freight, secured for $2.7 million back in August 2021.
