Northwind Logistics reported revenue of $4.9 million for the third quarter of 2025, an increase of 15 percent against the same period a year earlier. The company said the gain was driven almost entirely by its enterprise segment, which grew 30 percent while the consumer division contracted by roughly 5 percent. Chief executive Marta Delacroix described the quarter as the strongest since the company listed in March 2019.

The enterprise result was helped by three contracts signed in July, the largest of which was a five-year agreement with Bergstrom Manufacturing worth an estimated $12.8 million over its full term. Analysts had expected revenue between $4.6 million and $4.8 million, so the reported figure came in above the top of that range. Shares rose 6 percent in after-hours trading before giving back about half of the gain the following morning.

Operating costs also rose, though more slowly than revenue. Total operating expenses reached $3.6 million, up 10 percent year on year, with most of the increase attributable to headcount. Northwind Logistics employed 437 people at the end of September, compared with 381 twelve months earlier. Marta Delacroix said the company expected hiring to slow in the fourth quarter and that headcount would likely finish the year below 470.

The board declared a dividend of 11 cents per share, payable on 14 November to shareholders of record as of 31 October. It is the fourth consecutive quarter in which Northwind Logistics has paid a dividend, and the payout ratio now stands at approximately 21 percent of net income. Chief financial officer Peter Okonkwo said the board intended to keep the ratio below 31 percent while the company continues to invest in its network.

Northwind Logistics also disclosed a one-off charge of $495,000 relating to the closure of its Rotterdam warehouse, which ceased operations on 30 June. The company said the closure would reduce annual fixed costs by around $1.5 million from 2026 onward, and that the affected 43 staff had either been redeployed within the group or offered severance. Peter Okonkwo said the charge was fully reflected in the third-quarter accounts.

Looking ahead, the company guided to full-year revenue of between $18.5 million and $19.2 million, which would represent growth of between 13 and 19 percent on the prior year. Marta Delacroix cautioned that the fourth quarter is seasonally weaker for the consumer division and that the company was not assuming a recovery there before the second half of 2027. Its last acquisition was Palermo Freight for $2.7 million in August 2021.
