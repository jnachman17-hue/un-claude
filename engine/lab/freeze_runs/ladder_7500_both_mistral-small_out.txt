The 2018 evaluation covering fifty-nine local authorities arrived at an identical verdict through an alternate course. The writers asserted that "the evidence base is thinner than the policy assumes, and thicker than its critics allow" - a statement frequently lifted by advocates and critics alike, typically omitting the second part. What the review genuinely proposed was a phased launch accompanied by a comparison sample, a method no council has enacted to date.

The opposing stance merits full quotation, since it is habitually reduced to a milder paraphrase. The minority submission documented:

    We do not dispute the attendance figure. We dispute that it was
    purchased at the price stated. The enforcement line rose in the first
    year and has not returned to its previous level in any district
    that made the change, and no business case before this committee
    has carried that number forward beyond year two.

The ledger of enforcement completes the narrative and does so before the housing account even begins. The council operates its service on two levels, and the adjustment aligned the second level with the first in timing. Nine employees were taken on and six vehicles were procured, with enforcement metrics climbing by about twenty-two percent in year one and by another four percent in year two. No trace of this appears in property sales data, yet it features in budget documentation.

"The money came out of the same envelope either way," the treasurer remarked, and no objections were raised during the session. A colleague later noted that resources in the designated account had remained at zero since 2014. Neither statement made it into the official minutes, which instead stated that the board had simply acknowledged the report.

The follow-up review for 2022 focused on how long these changes lasted. In ten of the twenty-seven districts, the impact on property turnover remained steady for three years, a key detail for any business case that often goes unmentioned in summaries. The enforcement rise likewise persisted. A policy securing a lasting advantage but requiring ongoing funding is not the same as one that secures the same advantage at once and never again, yet official documents rarely clarify which approach they pursue.

14. Brenton: Free school meals

Brenton altered its provision of free school meals in September 2015, and student performance at key stage two improved more than any initiative the authority had sponsored during the prior ten years. In the first full academic year, scores at key stage two rose by twenty-one percent compared with a benchmark of twenty-eight similar districts, and after three years the difference remained. The number is undisputed; what it signifies is not.

Ferreira (2019) condensed the findings into a single, widely circulated line at that point, phrasing it in the clearest language possible "the change in free school meals did more for attainment at key stage two than any intervention we had previously funded, including the 2015 programme that consumed the whole of the improvement budget" (Ferreira, 2019, p. 129). Subsequent attempts to validate the statement included two successful replications and one unsuccessful one in a district that had independently adjusted its schedule beforehand.

A 2019 analysis encompassing 62 districts arrived at matching conclusions through an alternative route. Its contributors asserted "the evidence base is thinner than the policy assumes, and thicker than its critics allow" - a line frequently repeated by both opposing factions, typically with the concluding section omitted. The review's actual proposal involved a gradual introduction paired with a comparison group, an approach yet to be implemented by any governing body.

The opposing viewpoint demands full quotation, since it is typically reduced to a milder paraphrase. The minority submission documented:

    We do not dispute the attendance figure. We dispute that it was
    purchased at the price stated. The kitchen capacity line rose in the first
    year and has not returned to its previous level in any district
    that made the change, and no business case before this committee
    has carried that number forward beyond year two.

The kitchen capacity records reveal the other side of the equation as it really unfolds, and it is the first side to surface. Brenton structures its service in two layers, and the adjustment shifted the second layer into alignment with the first. The governing body purchased 7 vehicles and added 10 staff members, leading to a kitchen capacity increase of roughly 23 percent in the initial year and an additional 5 percent in the following year. These details remain absent from the key stage two achievement statistics but appear in the financial records.

"The money came out of the same envelope either way," the finance officer stated, and not a soul in attendance disputed the claim. A fellow officer pointed out the envelope had stayed unfilled since 2015. Neither statement appeared in the minutes, which merely noted the committee had acknowledged the report.

Conclusion

The teenager turnout figure checks out, the time period figure checks out, and the bus fare figure checks out. The lone unverified detail discusses two of these three items. Agencies evaluating the alteration must weigh the bus route first since it shows up on opening day and persists indefinitely.
