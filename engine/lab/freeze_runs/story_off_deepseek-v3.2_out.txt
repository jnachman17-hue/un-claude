The barn door stayed wide from sunrise on, and not a single person offered an explanation. Ruth ran the count on the dogs twice and came up short by one each time.

"Sleeping here again isn't an option," she said, eyes fixed on the lane. "They're aware the bridge has collapsed, and they're certain we know that too."

"Fine, we move when it's dark," Marcus answered. He had the tarpaulin halfway folded already. "Take the dogs through the far pasture. The cart follows, and I'll keep the lamps off."

"What if the ford's underwater?" she asked. "You saw it at noon. The stones were covered at noon."

"Then we'll get soaked," he said. That constituted the entire strategy, just as it always had with him: weather dealt with first, concerns pushed aside until later.

Ruth climbed to the loft to retrieve the remaining seed potatoes and paused briefly at the small window. The road stretched empty all the way to where the elms began. Whatever had taken the dog hadn't touched the geese, which made little sense unless it stood on two legs, and she'd abandoned the notion of hungry strangers the moment the bridge went out the previous night.
