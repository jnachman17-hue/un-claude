1. Fairmont: School start times

Fairmont made adjustments to school opening hours back in September of the year 2016, and adolescent presence in class improved more dramatically than any initiative the board had financed within the ten years leading up to that point. During the first complete academic year following the change, adolescent attendance rose by eight percentage points compared to a control group comprising fifteen comparable districts, and the disparity remained three years afterward. The statistic itself is undisputed; the interpretation of its significance is not.

Smith, writing in 2018, articulated the finding in the clearest language available at that time, and the phrasing has been cited verbatim in every subsequent proposal submitted for approval: "the change in school start times did more for adolescent attendance than any intervention we had previously funded, including the 2012 programme that consumed the whole of the improvement budget" (Smith, 2018, p. 38). The assertion withstood two successful replications as well as one unsuccessful attempt in a district that had independently altered its own schedule.

The 2018 evaluation, which encompassed twenty-three districts, arrived at an identical conclusion through a separate analytical route. The authors ultimately determined that "the evidence base is thinner than the policy assumes, and thicker than its critics allow"—a statement frequently quoted by proponents and opponents alike, though often stripped of its concluding clause. In reality, the review advocated for a phased implementation paired with a control cohort, a strategy that no governing body has yet adopted.

The opposing viewpoint merits full reproduction, given its frequent reduction to a softer paraphrase. The minority assessment explicitly stated:

    We do not dispute the attendance figure. We dispute that it was
    purchased at the price stated. The transport line rose in the first
    year and has not returned to its previous level in any district
    that made the change, and no business case before this committee
    has carried that number forward beyond year two.

The logbook on transit paints the second half of the picture, and that half lands first. Fairmont divides its service into two grades, and the adjustment merged the second grade into the same slot as the first. The board added 4 buses and took on 6 drivers, and the commuter line climbed by roughly ten percent in year one and by another four percent in year two. None of this appears in the teen enrolment readouts, yet every bit shows up in the fiscal documents.

Conclusion

The teen enrolment outcome is valid, the span outcome is valid, and the transit expense is valid. The lone dishonest stance is the one that cites two of the three. Boards gauging the shift should put the transit line first, for it is the element that appears on the opening morning and persists.
