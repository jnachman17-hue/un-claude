The barn had stood wide open since dawn, and not a soul had offered a reason for it. Ruth tallied the dogs a second time and found herself one missing on both counts.

"Staying another night here isn't possible," she said, eyes fixed on the lane. "They're aware the bridge is down, and they're aware that we know."

"Then we'll head out under cover of dark," Marcus answered. He had already begun folding the tarpaulin. "Take the dogs by way of the far pasture. I'll come after with the cart, keeping the lamps dark."

"What if the ford is flooded?" she pressed. "You checked at midday. The water covered the stones by then."

"Then we'll get soaked," he shrugged, and that ended the planning, the way it had always been with him - act first, worry later.

Ruth climbed to the loft for the remaining seed potatoes and paused by the small window. The road stretched bare to the row of elms. Whatever had snatched the dog had spared the geese, which hardly added up unless it moved on two feet, and she had given up on believing in famished drifters the night the bridge collapsed.
