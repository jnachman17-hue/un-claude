The barn door had been left ajar since sunrise and not a soul would explain why. Ruth tallied the canines twice and both counts came up one shy.

"We can't endure another night here," she remarked, eyeing the dirt path. "They're aware the bridge is demolished, and they know we're aware too."

"Then we depart at dusk," Marcus responded. He already had the tarp in motion. "You guide the hounds through the eastern field. I'll trail with the wagon and blackened lamps."

"What if the stream engulfs the crossing?" she pressed. "You observed it at twelve. The flow crested the stepping stones then."

"Then we soak," he declared, and that encapsulated his entire strategy - typical of him - weather priority before qualms.

Ruth ascended to the garret for the final sack of seed potatoes and paused beside the tiny pane. The trail stretched empty as far as the oak row. The culprit had snatched the dog yet spared the fowl, a puzzle unless it walked upright, a notion she dismissed after the bridge's collapse.
