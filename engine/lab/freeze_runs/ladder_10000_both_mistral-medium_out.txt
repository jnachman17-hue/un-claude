1. Fairmont: School start times

In September 2016, Fairmont adjusted its school schedules, producing a shift in teenage attendance unlike any initiative the district had backed in the prior ten years. Within the first complete academic year, attendance among adolescents rose by 8 percent relative to a benchmark of 15 comparable districts—and that advantage persisted unchanged even three years on. No one contests the measurement itself; the debate centers on what it signifies.

Smith's 2018 analysis framed the outcome with stark clarity, delivering a line that has since become a fixture in every subsequent funding proposal: "the change in school start times did more for adolescent attendance than any intervention we had previously funded, including the 2012 programme that consumed the whole of the improvement budget" (Smith, 2018, p. 38). That assertion held firm through two independent validations and one instance where a district's preexisting schedule adjustments rendered replication impossible.

A separate 2018 examination of 23 districts arrived at an identical verdict through distinct methodology. Its researchers determined that "the evidence base is thinner than the policy assumes, and thicker than its critics allow"—a statement now cited selectively, often with the latter half omitted, by proponents and critics alike. The study's actual proposal called for a gradual, controlled implementation with a comparison cohort, an approach no district has adopted to date.

The opposing viewpoint, frequently softened in retellings, deserves to be presented as originally written. The dissenting document explicitly noted:

    We do not dispute the attendance figure. We dispute that it was
    purchased at the price stated. The transport line rose in the first
    year and has not returned to its previous level in any district
    that made the change, and no business case before this committee
    has carried that number forward beyond year two.

The movement records reveal the earlier chapter of this account. Fairmont's operations split into two categories, yet the adjustment forced both into overlapping schedules. Four new vehicles were purchased by the authority, alongside the recruitment of six employees, which drove a roughly 10 percent climb in transport usage during the initial year and an additional 4 percent in the next. These shifts are absent from the youth participation data but fully documented in the financial records.

"The funds were drawn from the identical allocation regardless," stated the financial representative, a point that met no opposition in the room. Another official noted that this allocation had been exhausted since 2012. Neither statement found its way into the official minutes, which merely stated that the committee acknowledged the report.

The 2022 review introduced a critical dimension: time. In 10 out of 15 districts, the influence on youth participation remained just as strong after three years—a detail pivotal for justifying investment yet frequently omitted from overviews. The transport expansion likewise held steady. A strategy that secures lasting benefits through ongoing expenses differs fundamentally from one that achieves the same with a single expenditure, though documents seldom clarify this distinction.
